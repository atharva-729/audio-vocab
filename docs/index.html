      2 the models

      Two AI models that turn sounds and words into numbers

      Both turn a clip and a sentence into numbers (an embedding). Similar things land close, so words can search sound.

      EmbeddingGemma 2 (Gemma): a words model with ears

      • Google's text model with an audio "ear": audio becomes pseudo-words.
      • Strong on speech and meaning; the podcast search runs on it.

      CLAP: a sound model that learned captions

      • LAION's model trained on sounds paired with captions ("a dog barking").
      • Built to match sounds to descriptions; hears the voice itself.

      Why two? A second opinion: shared results are general, the rest are quirks. They turned out good at different things.

        What we measured, and the charts

        Probe = "is the information in the model?"

        A tiny classifier trained on the model's numbers, tested on unseen clips from new people. The model itself is unchanged.

        Zero-shot = "can a typed word find it?"

        No training: each clip gets the label of the nearest typed word, like a search box.

        Probe high, zero-shot low = "knows but can't name". Always compare against guessing.

        Fine distinctions: sets of similar sounds

        How to read: each row is a set of similar sounds the probe must tell apart (e.g. giggle vs chuckle). The dot is the probe's accuracy (blue = Gemma, orange = CLAP). The horizontal line through a dot is the range we're 95% sure the true accuracy lies in (shorter = more certain). The grey tick is guessing for that set. The further a dot sits to the right of its grey tick, the finer the hearing.

          What it all means

          Finding 1: Gemma hears sounds well, but words barely reach them. CLAP does both.

            Finding 2: laughter types are only roughly separable, and not "better" than birds

              Finding 3: emotion is in there, but unnameable

                Finding 4: genre words partly work; "classical" means tonal sound without drums

                    Finding 5: the podcast search's failures sit where the model is weak
