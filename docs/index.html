    Is it in the model vs can a word find it

    1 the story

    Why we did this

      The two models, and why there are two

      EmbeddingGemma 2 (Gemma)

      The model the podcast search used, from Google. It's basically a text model with an audio "ear" attached: audio is turned into pseudo-words and fed through the same brain that reads text. Strong on speech.

      CLAP

      An older model from LAION, trained specifically on sound clips paired with captions ("a dog barking", "rain on a roof"). Its whole job is matching sounds to short descriptions.

      Why use both? As a second opinion. If both models show the same thing, it's a fact about audio AI in general; if only one does, it's a quirk of that model. It mattered a lot: they're good at different things.

      2 the one idea behind every number

      Two questions we ask of every sound

      Each model turns a clip into a list of numbers. We ask two different questions of those numbers.

      Probe: "Is the information in there?"

      A probe is a tiny, simple classifier trained on top of the model's numbers. Show it labelled examples of "giggle" and "chuckle"; it learns where the line between them falls; then we test it on clips it has never seen, from different people. The big model is never changed. If the probe is accurate, the model's numbers contain the difference.

      Zero-shot: "Can a word find it?"

      No training at all. Type the word ("a person giggling"), turn it into numbers with the same model, and label each clip by whichever word it's closest to. That's exactly how a search box works. If zero-shot is accurate, words can reach that sound.

      Probe high, word high

      The model knows it, and you can search for it with words.

      Probe high, word low

      "Knows but can't name": the information is in the sound numbers, but typing the word doesn't find it.

      Probe low

      The model doesn't really hear the difference at all.

      Reading the numbers: accuracy = share of test clips labelled correctly. Always compare it with guessing: with two options guessing is right half the time, with fifty options almost never. Every test uses different recordings, speakers or artists than training, so no cheating by recognising the same microphone or person.
