# Designing results pages (any project)

When building a page that presents a project's results (e.g. a GitHub Pages write-up), design it as a pitch for a busy executive who cares about the problem and the result, not the implementation. Reference examples: https://atharva-729.github.io/clef-video-probe/ and https://atharva-729.github.io/base64-image-probe/

- **First screen, no scrolling:** the problem and the question in one line, then the approach in about 4 short bullets, with the main point visually highlighted. Keep fonts moderate, not huge headline type.
- **Then the data:** briefly what data was used, how and why, in bullets. Show the data side by side rather than behind tabs or toggles that make the reader click through.
- **Then straight to results:** the main table or chart, made self-explanatory, with a legend beside it.
- **Make results drillable:** clicking a summary cell or tile should open a box showing the individual items behind it (what was right and what was wrong). Keep it clean, not wordy.
- **Non-obvious points:** after each result, 1–2 (up to a handful) bullets covering what the numbers alone don't show, with key phrases highlighted for emphasis and skimmability.
- **Then a chart** (e.g. accuracy vs cost) that explains itself.
- **Everything else goes in an optional section below** ("That is the result. Everything below is the how."): the full story, method, caveats, cost and how to reproduce. The top part links down to it wherever a reader might want the explanation.
- **Rearrange, don't delete:** when restructuring an existing page, keep the content and move it into the optional section.
